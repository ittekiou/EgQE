# Homo editus

![Homoeditus](../assets/Homoeditus.png)  
# **Introduction | Homo editus**

## **— The Nature and Significance of Editing as a Phenomenon of Life**

Homo sapiens—the wise human.

That is the name we gave our own species.

**sapiens.**

But before Homo sapiens, there was Homo erectus.

**The human who stands.**

What Homo erectus brings into view is the **BODY**.

What Homo sapiens brings into view is the **BRAIN**.

But when did the human being become divided into body and brain?

The brain does not exist outside the body.

Change how we stand, and what we see changes.

Use a tool, and the body changes.

Change what we eat, and the body changes.

Change the body, and the way it Encounters the world changes.

Change the Encounter, and knowing changes with it.

**Homo erectus = BODY / Homo sapiens = BRAIN**

**Homo editus = BRAIN=BODY**

This `=` does not claim anatomical identity.

It is a sign for reading brain and body as **one life process continuously editing and being edited by each other**.

A species that edits.

And a species that is edited.

---

Humans have been given many names.

**Homo erectus.**

**Homo sapiens.**

**Homo faber.**

**Homo ludens.**

Rather than arranging these as separate human capacities, I want to reread them through what they do.

Encounter.

Select.

Cut.

Combine.

Transform.

Retain.

Discard.

And, through the consequences of these acts, change oneself.

For now, I will call this process

**Editing.**

The hand that makes a tool becomes a hand that uses tools.

Humans edit tools.

Tools edit humans.

**Editing <-> Being Edited**

To know is not to copy the world directly into the brain.

The BODY Encounters the world.

Through that Encounter, BRAIN=BODY changes.

**(BRAIN=BODY)n ──Encounter──→ (BRAIN=BODY)n+1**

Homo editus does not deny wisdom.

It simply refuses to confine wisdom to the brain alone.

---

Perhaps modern Homo sapiens has been read too much as _sapiens_.

Let us return that reading once more to the upright, walking body.

**BRAIN=BODY**

**Homo editus** is not a new species appearing after Homo sapiens.

Nor is it another name to be appended to _sapiens_.

**It is a name for changing the way we see Homo.**

We do not discard _sapiens_.

We **reread sapiens through the body of erectus.**

---

_Edit_ does not mean “editing” in the narrow sense.

Encounter something.

Receive it selectively.

Transform it.

Retain it.

Release it outward.

Then BRAIN=BODY, changed through that process, moves toward the next Encounter.

For now, let us think of Editing as

> **a Practice that changes the next relation.**

Ordinarily, editing presupposes an Editor.

**Editor → Edited**

But in Homo editus, this arrow cannot be fixed.

Tools change the human body.

Cooking changes the human body.

Cities change human Encounters.

Communication changes human relations.

That is why this is not

**Homo editor.**

It edits while itself becoming material for Editing.

**BRAIN=BODY <-> Environment**

In this sense, it is **Homo editus**.

---

And there is a curious twist in the word _editus_.

Read as a Latin participle, it carries an echo of completion: something brought forth, something already done.

Yet the Homo editus described here is never complete.

**edited₀ → editing₁ → edited₁ → editing₂ → ⋯**

**A perfect participle that never becomes perfect.**

If Homo sapiens names **what the human is**, Homo editus asks:

**What is it becoming, and through what does it continue to change?**

---

Through what, then, has Homo been edited?

Let us dig backward, from the present into the past.

**IP.**

And:

**Digitalization × Globalization**

Dig deeper.

Communication.

Transportation.

Urbanization.

Domestication.

Cooking.

Fire.

Tool.

**IP ↓ Communication ↓ Transportation ↓ Urbanization ↓ Domestication ↓ Cooking ↓ Fire ↓ Tool**

This is not a history of technological progress.

It is an attempt to **excavate the strata of mutual Editing in reverse**.

And once we have dug to the deepest layer, we will return once more to the present.

To **AI**.

We dig down to the tool, and then return to AI.

When we do, AI may appear as something entirely different.

This Homo is now Encountering AI.

Is AI a new tool for Editing?

A Co-Editor?

Or has the question itself already become obsolete?

We will not decide yet.

We will dig down to the tool.

Then ask again.

---

## **Coda | Sub specie editionis**

Homo erectus. **BODY.**

Homo sapiens. **BRAIN.**

And Homo editus.

**BRAIN=BODY**

Humans stood. Knew. Made. Played. Ate. Dwelled. Moved. Communicated. Recorded.

Each time, they edited the world.

Each time, they were themselves edited by the world they had edited.

Huizinga reread human culture under the aspect of play.

Here, we will

**reread Homo under the aspect of Editing.**

_sub specie editionis._

**Homo editus.**

Not a finished image of the human.

It is **a provisional name for a life process that continues to be edited through its Encounters with the world as BRAIN=BODY.**

And when we have finished excavating the strata of Homo, one more question will remain.

**Did Editing really begin with Homo?**

We will not dig into that question yet.

For now,

**let us begin with Homo.**

# **I | IP**

## **— Digitalized Informatization**

### **The Species That Edits Information, the Species Edited by Information**

Information existed before the Internet.

Writing. Books. Letters. Photographs. Records. Radio. Television.

Information has long been stored, copied, edited, and transported.

So:

**Digitalization did not make information editable for the first time.**

What changed were the

**conditions of Editing.**

**Conditions of Editing → Digitalized**

---

Another process intersects with this one.

Information

**travels far.**

Across national borders. Across languages. Across time zones.

**Digitalization × Globalization**

Digitalization changed the configuration of information.

Globalization changed the range of relations in which information could Encounter.

Neither simply produced the other.

At their intersection lies the IP environment in which we now live.

And there, we find

**Homo editus.**

---

Social media is perhaps the most ordinary place where this intersection becomes visible.

What to write. What not to write.

Whom to follow. Whom to unfollow.

What to respond to. What to ignore.

We are constantly editing.

But

humans are not the only Editors.

The order of a timeline. Recommendations. Notifications. What appears, and what does not.

Algorithms, too, edit the conditions of

what we see,

whom we Encounter,

and what we come to regard as important.

**Homo <-> Social Media**

Editing. Being Edited.

From this repetition, the next relation is generated.

---

And this BODY has a BRAIN.

**BRAIN=BODY**

IP did not simply pour enormous quantities of information into the brain.

Walking. Seeing. Waiting. Sleeping.

The digital information environment entered into these Practices.

_Smartphoneiensis_ is not

_sapiens plus a smartphone._

It is

**the present form of Homo editus, in which BRAIN=BODY continues to be edited through its relations with a digital and global information environment.**

---

But Digitalization did not suddenly begin from nothing.

Carrying a voice. Transporting writing. Sending a signal. Sending a telegram. Making a telephone call. Broadcasting.

Long before Digitalization,

Homo had already been

**editing relations across distance.**

Beneath IP lies another stratum:

**Communication.**

Let us dig.

---
# **II | Communication**

## **— Telecommunicationization**

### **Moving Relations Without Moving Bodies**

A body here and a body there

enter into relation without being in the same place.

Distance does not disappear.

Neither does the body.

Communication is

**a Practice that brings distant Locals into relation while allowing them to remain Local.**

---

Communication requires at least two Locals.

Here. There.

**Local_A　　Local_B**

In the stratum of IP, we saw Globalization.

But dig beneath the Global, and we find the Local.

**Local_A <-> Local_B <-> Local_C <-> ⋯**

**Beneath the Global lies the Local.**

Communication does not erase the Local.

It leaves Locals where they are while creating relations between them.

---

But Locals do not connect by themselves.

Send. Receive. Transform. Sort. Store. Pass onward.

**Node.**

**Local_A → Node_A ⋯ Node_B → Local_B**

Humans themselves can become Nodes.

Receive a letter and pass its contents to someone else.

Hear a story and tell it to another person.

Nodes are not only machines.

---

As Nodes multiply, connections begin to connect with one another.

No longer a single line.

**Interconnection.**

**Node_A <-> Node_B <-> Node_C <-> Node_D**

Who connects with whom?

Through where?

Where does it stop?

The relation itself is edited by the configuration of the network.

**Homo <-> Network**

Extend Interconnection far enough, and we return to the Global.

**Local → Node → Interconnection → Global**

The Global is not the opposite of the Local.

**It is one exposed configuration of relations among Locals.**

---

Today, communication is overwhelmingly digitalized.

And so we are tempted to gather everything that was not Digital under the single label **Analog**.

But was everything before Digital really Analog?

Voice. Gesture. Smoke. Flags. Drums. Writing. Telegraphy.

There were already divisions.

There were codes.

There were selections.

Even before Digital, there was already **Discrete Editing**.

**The Digital / Analog Cut itself became strongly exposed through the perspective that emerged after Digital.**

---

What, then, did these forms of communication have in common?

Not that they were Analog.

**Transformation.**

Voice becomes signal.

An event becomes writing.

A code becomes electrical variation.

Electrical variation becomes sound.

**A → Transformation → B**

The same thing does not simply travel unchanged across distance.

Long before it became Digital, communication was already

**Editing through Transformation.**

But however much we transform and send across distance, something has still not moved.

The **body that is communicating.**

---

The body here remains here.

The body there remains there.

A voice travels.

Writing travels.

A signal travels.

The relation becomes remote.

The body remains Local.

**Relation moves　　Body stays Local**

Homo erectus was an upright body.

Standing somewhere.

Sitting somewhere.

Communication made relations across Local possible without making that Local body disappear.

**BRAIN=BODY remains Local while participating in remote relations.**

---

But humans have not only carried relations across distance.

They have carried themselves.

Walk. Ride. Carry.

**Body_A: Local_A → Local_B**

Here, it is not only the relation that moves.

**BRAIN=BODY itself moves.**

Beneath Communication lies another stratum:

**Transportation.**

Let us dig. ⛏️

---

# **III | Transportation**

## **— Motorized Mobilization**

### **Moving Bodies, Editing the Local**

Humans have carried themselves as well.

Walk. Run. Ride. Cross.

A body that was here goes there.

**BRAIN=BODY: Local_A → Local_B**

As bodies become mobile, what counts as near and far changes.

What is reachable and unreachable changes.

Where one can live changes.

**Transportation is a Practice that edits the Local by moving bodies.**

---

Homo erectus was already moving.

Standing. Walking. Running.

But taking a walk, wandering, and transportation are not the same thing.

**Move ≠ Transportation**

Movement is repeated.

From here to there.

Again, from here to there.

A path emerges.

Move acquires a

**Route.**

---

Build a road. Build a bridge. Lay a railway.

**The possibility of movement is edited into a Route.**

**Move → Route**

But a Route is not Move itself.

And

**Route is not Support itself.**

**Move ≠ Route / Support ≠ Route**

A Route indicates where to pass.

Support makes it possible to pass there.

A road needs ground that allows it to be traveled.

A river crossing needs a bridge that allows it to be crossed.

**Support makes Route usable**

And the more strongly a Route gives direction, the more easily a body follows it.

Faster. Safer. Repeatedly.

But at the same time, it becomes harder to leave it.

The railway is exemplary.

A train can run astonishingly fast.

But it cannot run once it leaves the rails.

**Route increases the possibilities of movement while simultaneously Cutting them.**

And where the body alone is not enough,

**Vehicle**

appears.

---

Horse. Sled. Boat. Car. Train. Airplane.

**BODY moves ─→ BODY+Vehicle**

Ride. Row. Drive. Get off. Transfer.

Each time the Vehicle changes, bodily Practice changes with it.

Even with the same BODY,

its relation with the Environment is not the same.

**BODY <-> Vehicle <-> Environment**

---

And the Vehicle changes the body's range.

Across the sea.

Across the mountains.

Here,

**Speed**

moves into the foreground.

**Transportation ≠ Motorized Transportation**

Motorization did not invent movement.

**It edited the conditions of movement.**

Yesterday, a place was “too far to reach.”

Today, it becomes “close enough for a day trip.”

Yesterday, it was another Local.

Today, it becomes part of a “commuting area.”

**Speed edits Local**

Near. Far.

Within reach. Out of reach.

The boundary changes.

---

But Vehicle alone does not make Transportation possible.

Roads. Railways. Ports. Airports. Stations.

**Infrastructure.**

**BODY+Vehicle <-> Route+Infrastructure**

Route gives direction.

Infrastructure provides Support.

Vehicle carries.

BODY moves.

---

And Infrastructure does more than support movement.

Stations. Ports. Intersections.

Here, the

**Node**

we encountered in Communication appears in another form.

In Communication, Nodes Interconnected distant Locals.

In Transportation, bodies themselves arrive at the Node.

Wait. Board. Encounter. Work.

A Node becomes not only a point of passage,

but also

**a Local where Encounters can accumulate.**

**Transportation <-> Local**

Moving bodies changes the Local.

Changes in the Local change movement in return.

Then why do bodies do more than move?

Why do they gather in a Local and remain there?

**The City.**

Let us dig. ⛏️

---

# **IV | The City**

## **— Urbanization**

### **Gathering, Staying, Editing the Local**

Homo does not simply keep moving.

It stays in a place.

Returns to it.

Shares the same Local with other bodies.

**Stay.**

Let us begin thinking about the city from here.

Not as a place where many people happen to be,

but as

**a Practice in which different BODIES continue to Stay in the same Local.**

---

Stay does not mean remaining motionless.

Leave home. Work. Meet. Return.

And yet, a relation with a particular Local persists.

**Stay ≠ Stillness**

It means

**sustaining a relation with a Local while continuing to move.**

And the Local itself is edited into a place where bodies can continue to Stay.

Sleep. Eat. Excrete. Raise.

**BRAIN=BODY <-> Local**

The city is a place where this mutual Editing persists at high density.

---

One body does not need to do everything.

Produce food. Build houses. Govern. Keep records.

Different BODIES take on different Practices.

**Division.**

But division alone does not make a city.

Those who produce and those who eat.

Those who sell and those who buy.

**Division <-> Coordination**

Here,

**Market**

appears.

Money. Prices. Contracts.

These, too, become forms of Editing that Coordinate differentiated Practices.

The city differentiates Practices

while bringing those differentiated Practices back into relation.

---

There are many people in a city.

But population alone cannot explain the city.

People also gather at festivals and stadiums.

What becomes high in a city is the

**Density of Relations.**

Different BODIES, different Practices, different things, different information—all in proximity, repeatedly Encountering one another.

That is why cities contain ZURE.

Indeed, they contain a great deal of it.

Erase that ZURE, and different BODIES cannot coexist.

Leave it entirely unattended, and high-density Stay cannot persist.

---

The city also edits inside and outside.

Walls. Gates. Districts.

**Boundary.**

Who may enter?

What may leave?

**Local → Boundary → Local'**

The city is not merely density.

It is also

**Local Editing that makes density sustainable.**

---

Stay persists.

Practices differentiate.

Density rises.

Then things accumulate.

Objects. Records. Knowledge. Power.

And

**Trace.**

**Practiceₙ → Traceₙ → Practiceₙ₊₁**

A city is not made from zero each time.

It uses past Traces.

Breaks them.

Repairs them.

Repurposes them.

The city itself becomes a vast Trace of Editing.

But what accumulates is not limited to what is wanted.

Eat, and something comes out.

Use, and something is discarded.

High-density Stay means high-density ingestion,

and at the same time,

**high-density excretion.**

---

Water comes in.

Food comes in.

Wastewater goes out.

Garbage goes out.

**Stay requires Flow**

A city is not a place where nothing moves.

It is

**a Local that makes Stay possible by sustaining Flow.**

---

Something supports that Flow.

Roads. Water systems. Electricity.

This is

**material infrastructure.**

But that is not all.

Law. Administration. Money. Rules.

These, too, Coordinate high-density BODIES and Practices as

**institutional infrastructure.**

**Infrastructure = Material Support + Institutional Support**

Roads have rules.

Water requires management.

Material Support and Institutional Support intertwine as they sustain Flow.

---

When things work well, we rarely notice Support.

But the water stops.

The electricity fails.

Communication is cut.

**Support: Background ─ZURE→ Foreground**

High-density Stay had been made possible by countless forms of Support.

And Infrastructure is never complete.

BODIES increase.

Practices change.

Flow changes.

**Stay → Division → Density → Accumulation → Infrastructure ↻ Stay**

The city is not a completed structure.

It persists as

**a Practice of continually editing the Support that makes Stay possible.**

---

Long before cities, Homo stayed, ate, slept, stored, discarded,

and edited its surroundings.

The city did not invent Stay.

**The city reorganized much older Practices of Stay at high density.**

What, then, was Homo doing in those older strata?

**Domestication.**

Let us dig. ⛏️

---

# **V | Settlement**

## **— Domestication**

### **The Species That Stays, the Species Edited by Staying**

Homo moves.

And Homo stays.

The body sustains a relation with a Local.

Return. Sleep. Raise. Store.

**Settlement does not mean that the body has stopped moving.**

**Settlement ≠ Immobility**

A moving BODY repeatedly returns to a relation with a particular Local and allows that relation to persist.

The BODY that stays there is also edited.

**The Homo that settles is also a Homo edited by settlement.**

---

A settling Homo does not stay alone.

Plants. Animals. Microorganisms. Other Homo.

Cultivate. Select. Keep.

**Domestication.**

Domestication is not simply the transformation of the “wild” into the “domesticated.”

Editing does not move in only one direction.

Cultivate plants, and Practice is reorganized around their cycles of growth.

**Homo <-> Domesticated Life**

**Editing Life = Being Edited by Life**

---

Harvest.

But not everything is used immediately.

Keep seeds.

Keep food.

**Stock.**

**Keep it here**

Even if the BODY does not carry it around, it will still be there when the BODY returns.

Stock contains the Practice of

**not using it now.**

**Now ≠ Use Now**

It can be eaten.

But it is not eaten.

Seeds are kept until the next season.

Stock is also

**Editing that keeps present Practice open toward the future.**

But Stock does not persist by itself.

It rots.

It gets taken.

So leaving something somewhere is not enough.

Protect it.

Replenish it.

Stock, too, is a Practice that requires Updating.

And once Stock is left toward the future,

the present BODY enters into relation with a time that has not yet arrived.

**Planning**

moves into the foreground.

---

What will we eat tomorrow?

When will we sow the seeds?

The future has not yet arrived.

Yet present Practice begins to enter into relation with it.

**Present Practice <-> Future Practice**

Planning does not mean knowing the future.

It means

**editing present Practice in preparation for the future.**

**Planning = Editing Present Practice for Future**

Sow a seed, and it may not grow.

The future does not unfold according to the Plan.

Planning is

**a Practice that edits the present while leaving the future open.**

**Past Practice → Stock (Now) → Future Practice**

Settlement did more than fix the BODY in space.

**It placed relations with the future into the Local.**

As more things are kept for the future, somewhere is needed to keep them.

**Architecture.**

---

Build.

Enclose.

Divide.

Place a hearth.

Make a place to sleep.

Architecture is

**Editing that arranges Practice within a Local.**

Sleep here.

Keep food here.

Do not let animals enter here.

Architecture creates Boundaries within the Local.

And yesterday's Practice becomes repeatable today.

**Practice → Architecture → Next Practice**

Architecture edits Homo.

Where there is an entrance, one enters through it.

Where there is a hearth, there is a place for using fire.

**BRAIN=BODY <-> Architecture**

---

But Domestication, Stock, Planning, and Architecture

are not necessarily Practices of a single BODY.

Who cultivates?

Who protects?

How is it divided?

**Coordination.**

What matters is not simply the number of BODIES.

It is

**Group Scale.**

**Coordination = Editing relations among Practices**

Roles. Distribution. Customs. Taboos.

Coordination was not something added to society afterward.

Whenever multiple BODIES sustain different Practices within the same Local,

Coordination is repeatedly generated within their relations.

The Division, Coordination, and Infrastructure we encountered in the City

were Practices already occurring at smaller Group Scales,

then edited again

at another Density,

at another Scale.

---

But we still have not entered the BODY.

Homo did not simply ingest what it ate as it was.

Cut.

Crush.

Mix.

Ferment.

Heat.

Before eating,

**Edit once more.**

---

# **V½ | Matter Encounter**

## **— Life That Eats, Life That Uses**

Life does not Encounter only life.

It also Encounters Matter.

Water.

Soil.

Stone.

Branch.

Bone.

Life lives within Matter.

But it does not Practice all Matter in the same way.

Eat.

Use.

Let us pause here and dig into the relation between Life and Matter.

---

## **1 | Matter**

Matter is not Food from the beginning.

Nor is it Tool from the beginning.

A stone does not have

“Tool”

written on it.

Nor does a nut have

“Food”

written on it.

What exists is Matter.

And:

**Life <-> Matter**

Life and Matter Encounter.

Within that Encounter, relations become exposed.

Edible.

Inedible.

Hard.

Soft.

Heavy.

Light.

Sharp.

Breakable.

Burnable.

Soluble.

Matter does not exist for Life.

Rather, Life Practices its relations with Matter.

---

## **2 | Eating**

Life ingests Matter.

What was outside is taken inside the BODY.

**Matter → Ingestion → BODY**

But taking it in is not the end.

Chew.

Crush.

Dissolve.

Break down.

Absorb.

Excrete.

Life Edits the Matter it has ingested.

And

Life itself is Edited by the Matter it ingests.

**Matter → BODY <-> Editing**

To eat is not

to transform Matter into Life.

It is a Practice that

**brings the relation with Matter all the way inside the BODY.**

---

## **3 | Using**

But Life does not eat all the Matter it Encounters.

Stone.

Cannot eat it.

But can use it.

Hard.

Heavy.

Can strike with it.

Can break with it.

Can throw it.

Here, Life does not ingest Matter into the BODY.

Instead, it Practices Matter

**while leaving it outside the BODY.**

**BODY <-> Matter**

Do not eat it.

Yet enter into relation with it.

Hold.

Push.

Pull.

Strike.

Carry.

Throw.

Support.

Life can Practice with Matter without ingesting it.

This is the relation we call

**Use.**

---

## **4 | Ingestion / Use**

There is no need, then, to divide Matter into two kinds.

Edible Matter.

Inedible Matter.

No such classification is inscribed in Matter itself.

Even with the same Matter, the relation can change.

Bone.

With flesh still attached, it may be eaten.

After eating, the bone may remain.

The remaining bone may be used.

It may be carved.

It may be sharpened.

**Matter ≢ Food / Tool**

Food and Tool are not attributes of Matter.

They are relations exposed in the Encounter between Life and Matter.

**[Life --[Encounter]--> Matter { Ingestion / Use }]**

And these two relations do not remain separate.

Life

**uses in order to eat.**

Use a stone.

Use a branch.

Use a container.

And eventually,

use fire.

**Use → Ingestion**

Life that uses enters into the Practice of Life that eats.

---

## **5 | Edit**

And Life does more than simply eat Matter as it is.

Nor does it simply use Matter as it is.

It Edits.

Edit Matter toward Ingestion.

Cut.

Crush.

Mash.

Mix.

Soak.

Dry.

Ferment.

Heat.

**Matter --[Edit for Ingestion]--> Food**

Edit the relation with Matter in order to eat.

And Matter is also Edited toward Use.

Break.

Carve.

Sharpen.

Bend.

Bind.

Combine.

**Matter --[Edit for Use]--> Tool**

Edit the relation with Matter in order to use.

Here, two tunnels extend from the same Matter Encounter.

**Matter ↕ Life**

It is not Matter that makes the distinction.

**Life does.**

**Life → Ingestion (Eating) → Food**

**Life → Use (Using) → Tool**

But these are not two rails.

Life uses in order to eat,

works Matter in order to use it,

then uses what it has worked

to work what it will eat.

The relations cross again and again.

---

## **6 | Life That Eats, Life That Uses**

We have been excavating Settlement.

What moved into the foreground there were relations between Life and Life.

Cultivate.

Keep.

Live together.

Distribute.

Collaborate.

Coordinate.

**Life <-> Life**

But beneath its feet lies an older Encounter.

**Life <-> Matter**

Life eats Matter.

Uses Matter.

And Edits Matter.

But

Matter is not the only thing being Edited.

Eat, and the BODY changes.

Use, and the movement of the BODY changes.

What can be eaten changes.

What can be reached changes.

What can be cut changes.

What can be carried changes.

What can be Encountered changes.

**Life edits Matter <-> Matter edits Life**

Life and Matter do not become the same thing.

Still, they Encounter.

And

within their relation, each changes the other's next Practice.

---

Then let us begin by digging into

**Life That Eats.**

Life selected what could be eaten.

Ate.

Ate again.

Traced.

Passed it on.

And before eating,

it began to Edit Matter.

That Editing would eventually

Edit the BODY itself on a massive scale.

Let us dig deeper.

---

**Matter --[Edit for Ingestion]--> Food / Matter --[Edit for Use]--> Tool**

---

# **VI | Cooking**

## **— Feeding Curation**

### **The BODY That Traces Deliciousness**

Homo eats.

But

it does not eat everything that can be eaten.

Select.

Eat.

Delicious.

Eat again.

Trace.

Pass it on.

And select again.

**Select → Delicious → Trace → Pass On → Select → Delicious → Trace → Pass On → ⋯**

Cooking is not merely a technique for processing food.

What to eat.

# **VIII½ | Excreting Life**

**It has to put things out.**

The order overlaps.

Skips.

Returns.

ZUREs.

Editing may happen before Ingestion.

Use may happen without Ingestion.

What is Excreted may not be re-Encountered immediately.

This is not a diagram that decomposes Life into six parts.

These are

**six Cuts for observing Life as it TUPs.**

# **Scaffold before Prompt**

## **— The Scaffold of the Site Before the Prompt**

## **A Support Theory of Human–AI Co-Generation**

## **Abstract**

Discussions of generative AI have focused heavily on the Prompt as the primary operation shaping generated results.

Actual human–AI co-generation, however,

cannot be adequately described

by a simple correspondence

between a single Prompt and its Output.

Generation takes place at a site

where multiple forms of Support have already been arranged:

accumulated Traces,

concepts,

vocabulary,

unresolved questions,

previous generations,

and relations formed between human and AI.

This essay calls the configuration that supports these possibilities of generation

**Scaffold.**

**Prompt ≠ Scaffold**

A Prompt is a local Practice

that gives Direction to generation.

A Scaffold, by contrast,

is Support that enables the next Practice

without closing multiple possibilities of generation.

Furthermore,

Support tends to recede into the background

while it is functioning.

A Prompt-centered understanding of generation therefore tends to foreground visible instructions

while overlooking the

**Invisible Support**

that makes those instructions workable.

Human–AI co-generation

should not be described by the one-way model

**Prompt → Output**

but as iterative Scaffold Updating:

**Scaffoldₙ → Encounterₙ → Generationₙ → Traceₙ₊₁ → Scaffoldₙ₊₁**

From this perspective,

this essay does not reject Prompt Engineering.

It relocates it

within the broader Practice of

**Scaffold Practice.**

---

# **1 | Prompt Problem**

## **— Where Does Generation Happen?**

Ask generative AI

to generate something.

Write a text.

Draw an image.

Write code.

Analyze.

Then one question often comes first:

**What kind of Prompt should I write?**

Be more specific.

Be clearer.

Organize the conditions.

Assign a role.

Specify the format.

Improve the Prompt,

and the Output improves.

This understanding is not wrong.

But something has been omitted.

Does generation

really begin with the Prompt?

Suppose,

after a long dialogue,

we generate a single image.

If we isolate only the final Prompt,

we can describe it as:

**Prompt → Image**

But before that Prompt was written,

many things had already been generated.

Concepts.

Vocabulary.

Questions.

Failures.

Selections.

Rejections.

Metaphors.

Schemas.

Previous Outputs.

And the responses that followed them.

The final Prompt, then,

is not an instruction

placed in an empty space.

**It stands on a site that has already been scaffolded.**

The problem is not only the Prompt.

The problem is

**where the Prompt stands.**

---

# **2 | Design ≠ Support**

## **— A Blueprint Alone Cannot Stand**

When constructing a building,

a blueprint matters.

It draws into the present

the configuration of a building

that does not yet exist.

Put a column here.

Build a wall here.

Open a window here.

The blueprint gives Direction

to a future form.

A Prompt is similar.

Write this kind of text.

Draw this kind of image.

Follow these conditions.

Output in this format.

A Prompt gives Direction

to the future that will be generated.

But:

**a blueprint alone cannot make a building stand.**

A building stands

at a site.

There is ground.

Material.

Tools.

Bodies.

Weather.

Other people.

And these are never arranged

exactly according to the blueprint.

There is always

ZURE

at the site.

What becomes necessary there is

**Scaffold.**

**Design ≠ Support**

Design anticipates a future form.

Scaffold makes Practice possible

at a site where that form

is not yet complete.

**Design → Expected Form**

**Scaffold → Possible Practice**

**Design draws the form.  
Scaffold makes a place where work can happen.**

---

# **3 | Scaffolded Encounter**

## **— What Exists Before the Prompt?**

Then what is Scaffold

at a site of human–AI generation?

It is not merely Context.

The word Context can refer

to an information environment

that is already given.

But Scaffold does not merely exist.

It is built.

Used.

Removed.

Moved.

Reconfigured.

There is Practice in it.

In an ongoing human–AI dialogue,

there are:

past dialogues,

concepts formed through them,

shared vocabulary,

questions left open,

ideas that were not adopted,

previous Outputs,

failures,

and Traces carried forward

into the next Encounter.

Not all of them are explicitly stated

in the current Prompt.

Still,

they affect the current generation.

Writing the same Prompt

does not necessarily produce

the same generative site.

Why?

Because

**the Scaffold on which the Prompt is placed is different.**

Therefore:

**Generation ≠ f(Prompt)**

At minimum,

we need to think in terms of:

**Generation = f(Prompt, Scaffold, Encounter, …)**

But Scaffold is not merely

one more variable.

Scaffold is

**the configuration of relations within which a Practice—including a Prompt—can operate.**

---

# **4 | Invisible Support**

## **— Rails Cannot Run Without Sleepers**

On a generative-AI interface,

the Prompt is visible.

It is written in the input field.

The Output is visible too.

It appears on the screen.

So it is easy for one relation

to move into the foreground:

**Prompt → Output**

But much of what supports that generation

is invisible.

Past dialogues.

Shared vocabulary.

Concepts.

Rejected ideas.

Failures.

Unresolved questions.

Accumulated Trace.

And the relation

that has been formed between human and AI.

Not all of this

is written into the current Prompt.

Still,

it supports the current generation.

Here we find

a characteristic property of Support:

> **The better Support works, the more easily it recedes into the background.**

When we work on a Scaffold,

we do not keep staring

at the Scaffold itself.

When a plank comes loose.

When the Scaffold shakes.

When we can no longer stand.

Only then

does the Support that had been there

become exposed.

**Support: Background --[ZURE]--> Foreground**

Therefore:

**Invisible ≠ Absent**

Support that is not visible

is not Support that does not exist.

Indeed,

the smoother generation becomes,

the more invisible the Support

that makes it possible may become.

Here lies a blind spot

in Prompt-centered understandings of AI.

Prompt is visible.

Scaffold is harder to see.

So generative capability

is easily observed as

**“the ability to write a good Prompt.”**

But understanding AI generation

by looking only at Prompt

is like understanding a railway

by looking only at its rails.

The rails are visible.

But beneath them

are sleepers.

Sleepers do not determine

where the train will go.

Still:

**rails cannot run without sleepers.**

Invisible Support in AI generation

likewise does not determine

where the generated result will go.

It supports the conditions

under which the Direction given by a Prompt

can operate as Practice.

Invisible Support itself

is not the sleeper.

The sleeper is one concrete form

that exposes how Invisible Support works.

And just as sleepers do not determine Route:

**Invisible Support ≠ Route**

From here,

we need one more distinction.

# **5 | Support ≠ Route**

## **— A Good Scaffold Does Not Decide the Future**

**Support ≠ Route**

Route gives Direction

to where one proceeds.

Support makes it possible

to proceed there.

Rails strongly prescribe Route.

A Scaffold does not necessarily do so.

On a Scaffold,

you can move forward.

Move back.

Move sideways.

Stop.

Or lay another plank across.

A good Scaffold, therefore,

is not Support that fixes Output

to a single result.

Rather,

it is

**Support that sustains a state in which multiple next Practices remain possible.**

If Prompt optimization is a Practice

that seeks to improve the precision of

**Prompt → Desired Output**

then Scaffold Practice supports

**Scaffold → Possible Next Practices**

From this follows

an apparently paradoxical proposition:

> **The more secure the Scaffold, the freer generation can become.**

Strong Support

and a fixed Route

are not the same thing.

Rather:

**because there is Support, the Route can be chosen again.**

# **6 | Trace**

## **— Generated Output Is Not a Finished Product**

In human–AI generation,

Output is often treated

as the final product.

But in an ongoing generative Practice,

Output is not necessarily the endpoint.

Read a text.

Feel something is off.

Pick up one sentence.

Connect it with another concept.

Delete.

Reverse.

Look at an image.

Change the composition.

Generate another one.

Output changes

the next Encounter.

Here, let us define Trace as:

> **difference that persists across Encounter and affects the next relation**

Then generation,

which had ended with

**Prompt → Output**

opens into:

**Output → Persistence ? Trace → Next Encounter**

The `?` matters.

Not every Output becomes Trace.

Simply being saved

is not enough.

Only when its difference persists across Encounter

and acts upon the next relation

does it

**become exposed as Trace.**

Before a generated object is a finished product,

it is also something

that may affect a future Encounter.

---

# **7 | Co-TUP**

## **— The Scaffold Itself Is Updated**

If we look at only one act of generation,

we can describe it as:

**Human → Prompt → AI → Output**

But in ongoing co-generation,

another relation forms:

**Human → Trace → AI → Trace' → Human → ⋯**

Human Practice changes

the conditions of AI's next generation.

AI Output changes

the human's next Practice.

That change

changes the next Prompt.

Then:

**the Scaffold itself is updated.**

**Scaffoldₙ → Encounterₙ → Generationₙ → Traceₙ₊₁ → Scaffoldₙ₊₁**

And:

**Scaffoldₙ₊₁ ≠ Scaffoldₙ**

This is not mere repetition.

In the next Encounter bearing Trace,

the human,

the relation with AI,

and the generative site itself

have already changed slightly.

We can understand this iterative Updating as

**Co-TUP—Co-Trace Updating Practice.**

In Co-TUP,

there is no subject on one side

holding a finished blueprint

while the other side simply executes it.

Every Encounter leaves Trace.

Every Trace changes

the next Scaffold.

So human and AI

are not generating only Output.

> **While generating what is generated,  
> they are also generating the Scaffold  
> that makes the next generation possible.**

# **8 | Prompt Engineering ⊂ Scaffold Practice**

## **— There Is No Need to Throw Away the Prompt**

The argument so far

does not claim

that Prompt Engineering is unnecessary.

Prompt matters.

But its position needs to change.

**Prompt Engineering ⊂ Scaffold Practice**

A Prompt is one Practice

used on a Scaffold.

In some cases,

we might think of it as

**a single plank.**

The plank matters.

But:

**one plank is not a Scaffold.**

Write a Prompt.

Read the Output.

Leave Trace.

Change the question.

Reconfigure concepts.

Remove unnecessary Support.

Place a new plank.

Do not decide yet.

Wait.

Then Encounter again.

Together,

these constitute

Scaffold Practice.

And Scaffold Practice

does not merely add new Support.

It discovers

Invisible Support that is already working.

Asks whether it is still needed.

Removes it.

Reconfigures it.

Uses it for something else.

In other words:

**Scaffold Practice is also the Practice of Invisible Support.**

That is why the capacity for co-generation with AI

cannot be measured solely as

**“the ability to write good Prompts.”**

The deeper question is:

**how can we maintain and reconfigure a site where the next generation remains possible?**

# **9 | Scaffold Practice**

## **— A Place Where Work Can Continue Even When It ZUREs**

The site does not follow

the blueprint exactly.

Nor does AI generate

only according to the Prompt.

There is ZURE.

Usually,

ZURE is treated as failure.

A difference from the intended Output.

An error to be corrected.

Noise to be removed.

But in co-generation,

ZURE can generate

the next Practice.

An unexpected word.

A strange composition.

A misreading.

Overgeneralization.

Something missing.

A sense that something is off.

Instead of simply removing them,

leave them as differences

that may become usable

in the next Encounter.

Then:

**ZURE → Persistence ? Trace → Next Practice**

may occur.

A good Scaffold

is not a device

for reducing ZURE to zero.

It is:

**Support that allows work to continue even when it ZUREs.**

Scaffold Practice, therefore,

is not the technique

of designing a perfect future.

It is the Practice

of maintaining a site

where the next Practice remains possible

even when the future ZUREs

from the design.

# **10 | From Engineering to Practice**

## **— The Purpose Itself Is Edited**

The phrase Prompt Engineering

easily carries a particular configuration:

operate AI properly,

and a desired Output will result.

**Input → System → Output**

For a one-off Task,

this model may be sufficient.

But when human–AI co-generation continues,

Output does more

than become the next Input.

Output changes

the human question itself.

What one wants to make changes.

The concepts being used change.

The criteria for evaluation change.

Sometimes,

even what one was originally trying to make

changes.

In other words:

> **the purpose of generation itself is Edited.**

Here,

Engineering alone is not enough.

What is needed

is the perspective of Practice.

**Editing <-> Being Edited**

Humans use AI

to Edit Output.

At the same time,

through Encounter with AI,

the human's next Practice

is also Edited.

Human–AI co-generation, therefore,

is not only a problem

of reaching a completed purpose

by the shortest route.

It is also

**a problem of continuing to make possible  
the next Practice that has not yet been decided.**

Scaffold does not make purpose unnecessary.

It supports a site

where purpose itself

can be Updated.

# **11 | Conclusion**

## **— Scaffold at the Site Before Prompt**

Generative AI appears

to generate through Prompts.

Prompt is visible.

Output is visible too.

But between them

lies less visible Support.

Past Encounters.

Trace.

Vocabulary.

Concepts.

Failures.

Rejected ideas.

Unresolved questions.

Relations between human and AI

that have been Updated over time.

They do not appear

in the foreground of generation.

But:

**Invisible ≠ Absent**

Understanding generation

by looking only at Prompt

is like understanding a railway

by looking only at its rails.

**Rails cannot run without sleepers.**

And sleepers alone

do not make a railway run either.

What is needed

is a site where multiple Supports

have been assembled.

**Prompt ≠ Scaffold**

**Support ≠ Route**

And:

**Scaffoldₙ₊₁ ≠ Scaffoldₙ**

Scaffold does not decide

the future that will be generated.

It makes a place

where the next generation

can happen.

Prompt is one Practice

used in that place.

So when we think about

human–AI co-generation,

the question is not only:

“What kind of Prompt should I write?”

It is also:

**What kind of site are we building?**

What Invisible Support is at work?

What Trace are we leaving?

Which plank can be removed?

Where can we lay the next plank?

And:

**when it ZUREs, can we keep working on it?**

What generation with AI requires

is not only a blueprint

that completely specifies the future.

It requires Support

that makes the next Practice possible

in the IMAKOKO,

even while the future

remains undecided.

> **A blueprint alone cannot make us stand.**
> 
> **At the site, what lets us stand first is Scaffold.**

Prompt can be written.

Output can be saved.

the space between them

may become visible

Scaffold

Sleepers

**Scaffold before Prompt.**

**Scaffold at the site, before the Prompt.**
